Six Pricing Models, Explained

Per-Seat / Per-User
Most Common
Charge per user, per month
How it works
MRR = Number of users × Price per seat
Example: 50 users × $15/seat = $750/mo

Pros

  • Predictable revenue per account
  • Simple for buyers to understand
  • Natural expansion as teams grow

Cons

  • Discourages adoption within org
  • Seat sharing / login abuse
  • Revenue caps when team is fully deployed
Who does it

Slack — per-user pricing encourages broad adoption, then upgrades for advanced features. Figma — per-editor seat with free viewers.

PLG Fit
Model MRR →
Usage-Based
Fastest Growing
Pay for what you consume
How it works
MRR = Units consumed × Price per unit
Example: 500K API calls × $0.001 = $500/mo

Pros

  • Zero barrier to start — pay as you go
  • Revenue scales with customer success
  • Built-in expansion revenue

Cons

  • Revenue is unpredictable month-to-month
  • Hard for buyers to forecast spend
  • Churn risk during low-usage periods
Who does it

Twilio — per-API-call pricing tied directly to customer value. Snowflake — compute credits consumed. AWS — pay for what you use.

PLG Fit
Model pricing →
Tiered / Feature-Gated
Most Flexible
Good-Better-Best plans gated by features
How it works
3–4 tiers, each unlocking more features
Starter $29 → Pro $79 → Business $199 → Enterprise custom

Pros

  • Captures different willingness-to-pay
  • Clear upgrade path and upsell triggers
  • Familiar structure buyers expect

Cons

  • Feature packaging is hard to get right
  • Can feel arbitrary ("why is SSO enterprise-only?")
  • Requires constant iteration
Who does it

HubSpot — Free, Starter, Pro, Enterprise with escalating features. Notion — Free for personal, Team, Business, Enterprise.

PLG Fit
Model tiers →
Freemium
PLG Standard
Free tier + paid upgrade
How it works
Free tier with usage or feature limits
Users upgrade when they hit the wall
Typical: 2–5% of free users convert to paid

Pros

  • Massive top-of-funnel — zero friction
  • Product sells itself through usage
  • Viral loops and word-of-mouth

Cons

  • Free users cost money to support/host
  • Low conversion if "wall" is too generous
  • Devalues product perception
Who does it

Dropbox — 2 GB free, upgrade for storage. Calendly — free for 1 event type. Loom — free with video limits.

PLG Fit
Model pricing →
Flat-Rate
Simplest
One price, everything included
How it works
Single plan: $X/month for everything
No tiers, no per-seat, no usage metering
Example: Basecamp — $349/mo, unlimited users

Pros

  • Dead simple to communicate
  • No pricing confusion or "gotchas"
  • Attracts value-conscious buyers

Cons

  • Leaves money on table from power users
  • No natural expansion revenue
  • Hard to serve both SMB and enterprise
Who does it

Basecamp — one flat price, unlimited users. Buffer (formerly) — simplified to one-price model for transparency.

PLG Fit
Model pricing →
Hybrid / Per-Seat + Usage
Advanced
Platform fee + variable usage component
How it works
Base platform fee + usage overage
Example: $99/mo base + $0.01 per transaction
Or: per-seat base + usage metering on top

Pros

  • Predictable base + upside from usage
  • Aligns price with multiple value dimensions
  • Captures enterprise and SMB

Cons

  • Complex to communicate on a pricing page
  • Billing system complexity
  • Harder to forecast for buyers
Who does it

Stripe — transaction fees (usage) + monthly platform add-ons. Intercom — seat-based + resolution-based pricing.

The 4-Tier Builder Framework

Structure your plans around buyer intent, not feature count

Each tier should have a clear job: attract, convert, grow, or land enterprise. Here's the framework top SaaS companies use.

Tier 1
Free / Trial
$0 / 14-day trial
Job: Remove all friction. Let users experience the core value.
  • Core workflow only — no extras
  • Usage cap or time limit
  • Single user / personal use
  • Community support only
  • Watermark or "Powered by" badge
Tier 2
Starter / Pro
$19–$79/mo
Job: Convert activated free users. The "no-brainer" upgrade.
  • Everything in Free, plus:
  • Higher or no usage limits
  • Key integrations
  • Remove branding
  • Email support
  • This is where most revenue starts
Tier 3
Business / Team
$99–$299/mo
Job: Grow revenue from successful customers. Expansion tier.
  • Everything in Starter, plus:
  • Team collaboration features
  • Advanced analytics / reporting
  • Priority support + SLA
  • Custom branding / white-label
  • This is your margin driver
Tier 4
Enterprise
Custom / "Contact Sales"
Job: Land large accounts. Justify a sales team.
  • Everything in Business, plus:
  • SSO / SAML / SCIM
  • Custom contracts and SLAs
  • Dedicated success manager
  • Audit logs, compliance, SOC 2
  • This is your ACV multiplier

Feature packaging rules of thumb

Where you put each feature determines your upgrade triggers. Get this wrong and you either give away too much or gate too aggressively.

Feature Type Where to Put It Why
Core value Free tier Users must experience the "aha moment" to convert
Convenience Starter Nice-to-haves that remove friction (integrations, export)
Collaboration Business Team features expand seats and drive expansion revenue
Admin & Security Enterprise SSO, audit logs, compliance — required by procurement
Volume / Limits Graduated Natural upgrade trigger as usage grows
Support level Graduated Community → Email → Priority → Dedicated

06

7 Pricing Mistakes That Kill Margins

1

Pricing based on cost, not value

You calculated your hosting costs, added a margin, and called it pricing. But your customer doesn't care what it costs you to run — they care what the outcome is worth to them.

Fix: Ask "What would this be worth if it worked perfectly?" and price at 10–20% of that value.
2

Too many tiers and add-ons

5 plans, 3 add-ons, a usage component, and a seat multiplier. By the time the buyer figures out their price, they've already bounced to a competitor with a simpler page.

Fix: 3–4 tiers max. One value metric. If your pricing page needs a calculator, it's too complex.
3

Giving away the "aha moment" for free forever

Your free tier is so generous that users never hit a reason to upgrade. You have 100K free users and 200 paying customers.

Fix: Free tier should deliver the core value loop once or twice, then create natural friction. Usage limits > feature gates.
4

Never raising prices

You set your price at launch, it's been 3 years, you've 10x'd the product, and the price hasn't moved. You're leaving 30–50% revenue on the table.

Fix: Review pricing every 6–12 months. Grandfather existing customers if needed, but raise for new signups.
5

Hiding the price

"Contact Sales" on every tier. In a PLG world, buyers expect transparent pricing. Hidden prices signal "expensive" and kill self-serve conversion.

Fix: Show prices for all tiers except true enterprise (custom contracts). "Starting at" is fine for usage-based.
6

Discounting to close every deal

Your sales team gives 30%+ discounts on every deal. Customers expect it, renewals require it, and your ACV shrinks every quarter.

Fix: Cap discounts at 15–20% for annual commitment only. Never discount the first deal — add more value instead.
7

Same price for all customer segments

A 5-person startup and a 5,000-person enterprise pay the same $49/month. The enterprise gets 100x the value and would gladly pay 20x the price.

Fix: Segment by company size, usage, or value received. Value-based pricing means different prices for different outcomes.
07

Pricing Psychology That Works

⚓

Anchoring

Show the most expensive plan first (or a high "compare at" price) to make mid-tier feel reasonable. The first number buyers see shapes every comparison after.

Enterprise: $599 → Pro: $99 → Starter: $29
Pro feels like a steal compared to Enterprise.
🎯

Decoy Effect

Add a plan that's intentionally less attractive to make your target plan look like the obvious choice. The decoy exists to be compared against, not purchased.

Basic: $29 (5 users) → Pro: $79 (unlimited users) → Plus: $69 (10 users)
Plus is the decoy — why pay $69 for 10 when $79 gets unlimited?
💰

Charm Pricing vs. Round Numbers

$9.99 works in B2C. In B2B SaaS, round numbers ($49, $99, $199) signal quality and reduce cognitive load. Use $X9 for entry tiers, round for premium.

B2C / SMB: $9.99/mo (bargain signal)
B2B / Mid-market: $99/mo (quality signal)
🚀

"Most Popular" Badge

Highlight your target plan with a "Most Popular" or "Recommended" badge. Social proof reduces decision fatigue and nudges buyers toward the plan you want them on.

Starter — Pro ★ MOST POPULAR — Enterprise
70% of signups should land on this plan.
📅

Annual Framing

Frame annual plans as "2 months free" instead of "17% off." Loss aversion makes "free months" feel more valuable than an equivalent percentage discount.

Weak: Save 17% with annual billing
Strong: Get 2 months free — pay for 10, get 12
🔒

Loss Aversion Triggers

During trials, show users what they'll lose when the trial ends. "Your 3 dashboards and 45 reports will be locked" is more motivating than "Upgrade to keep access."

Weak: "Upgrade to Pro for more features"
Strong: "Your 12 saved workflows will be paused in 3 days"
