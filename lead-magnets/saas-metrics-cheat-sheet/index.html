Revenue Metrics

Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)
Revenue
The total predictable revenue from all active subscriptions in a given month. The north-star metric for subscription businesses.
Formula
MRR = Σ (Monthly subscription price × Active subscribers)
Good
MoM growth > 10%

Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)
Revenue
MRR annualized. Used for enterprise SaaS or annual contracts. The standard metric investors use for valuation.
Formula
ARR = MRR × 12
Good
YoY growth > 100% (early) / > 40% (growth)

Calculate →
Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)
Revenue
Revenue generated per account per month. Tracks pricing effectiveness and expansion revenue over time.
Formula
ARPU = MRR / Total active accounts
Good
Increasing QoQ (expansion > churn)

Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
Revenue
Measures revenue kept + expanded from existing customers, net of churn and contractions. The single best health indicator for SaaS.
Formula
NRR = (Start MRR + Expansion - Contraction - Churn) / Start MRR × 100
Good
> 110% (best-in-class: 120%+)

02

Customer Metrics

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
Customer
The total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship. Critical for understanding how much you can spend to acquire customers.
Formula (Simple)
LTV = ARPU / Monthly churn rate
Good
LTV:CAC > 3:1
SeedGrowthScale
Calculate →
Customer Churn Rate
Customer
Percentage of customers who cancel in a given period. The silent killer of SaaS businesses — even small changes compound dramatically.
Formula
Churn Rate = (Customers lost in period / Customers at start) × 100
Good
< 5% monthly / < 7% annual (enterprise)
SeedGrowthScale
Track →
Revenue Churn Rate
Customer
Percentage of MRR lost to cancellations and downgrades. More telling than customer churn when you have variable pricing.
Formula
Revenue Churn = (MRR lost in period / MRR at start) × 100
Good
< 2% monthly (negative = best-in-class)
GrowthScale
Track →
Expansion Revenue Rate
Customer
Revenue growth from existing customers via upsells, cross-sells, and plan upgrades. The cheapest revenue you can get.
Formula
Expansion Rate = (Expansion MRR / Starting MRR) × 100
Good
> 20% of new revenue from expansion

Unit Economics

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
Unit Econ
Total cost of acquiring one customer, including all sales and marketing spend. The denominator of the most important SaaS ratio.
Formula
CAC = Total S&M spend / New customers acquired
Good
CAC < ⅓ of LTV
SeedGrowthScale
Compare →
LTV:CAC Ratio
Unit Econ
The efficiency of your growth engine. Below 3:1 means you're spending too much to acquire; above 5:1 may mean you're under-investing in growth.
Formula
LTV:CAC = Customer Lifetime Value / Customer Acquisition Cost
Good
3:1 to 5:1 (sweet spot)
GrowthScale
Calculate →
CAC Payback Period
Unit Econ
Months to recover the cost of acquiring a customer. Shorter = faster reinvestment into growth.
Formula
Payback = CAC / (ARPU × Gross margin %)
Good
< 12 months (SMB) / < 18 months (enterprise)
GrowthScale
Calculate →
Gross Margin
Unit Econ
Revenue minus cost of goods sold (hosting, support, infrastructure). SaaS should have structurally high margins.
Formula
Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100
Good
> 70% (best-in-class: 80%+)

Market Sizing

Total Addressable Market (TAM)
Market
The total revenue opportunity if you captured 100% of the market. Used for investor pitch and strategic direction.
Formula (Bottom-up)
TAM = Total potential customers × Annual contract value
VC bar
> $1B for venture-scale
SeedGrowth

Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
Market
The portion of TAM you can realistically serve with your current product, geography, and go-to-market.
Formula
SAM = TAM × % reachable with your product/geography
Typical
10–40% of TAM
SeedGrowth
Calculate →
Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
Market
Your realistic near-term capture — the portion of SAM you can win given competition, resources, and brand awareness.
Formula
SOM = SAM × Expected market share %
Typical
1–5% of SAM in year 1–3

Prioritization Frameworks

RICE Score
Priority
Intercom's framework for objective feature prioritization. Balances reach, impact, confidence, and effort to produce a comparable score.
Formula
RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
Usage

Priority

Customer Satisfaction

Net Promoter Score (NPS)
Satisfaction
The gold standard for customer loyalty. Measures likelihood to recommend on a 0–10 scale. Predicts growth and retention.
Formula
NPS = % Promoters (9–10) − % Detractors (0–6)
Good
> 30 (SaaS avg) / 50+ (great) / 70+ (world-class)
GrowthScale
Calculate →
Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT)
Satisfaction
Measures satisfaction with a specific interaction or experience. Best used after support tickets, onboarding, or feature releases.
Formula
CSAT = (Satisfied responses / Total responses) × 100
Good
> 75% (SaaS avg: 78%)

Customer Effort Score (CES)
Satisfaction
Measures how easy it was for a customer to accomplish a task. Low effort = higher retention. The strongest predictor of repurchase.
Formula
CES = Avg. score on "How easy was it?" (1–7 scale)
Good
> 5.5 / 7 (lower effort = better)

Growth Efficiency

SaaS Quick Ratio
Growth
Measures the balance between MRR gains (new + expansion) and losses (churn + contraction). Are you growing efficiently or plugging a leaky bucket?
Formula
Quick Ratio = (New MRR + Expansion MRR) / (Churned MRR + Contraction MRR)
Good
> 4 (healthy) / > 2 (minimum viable)
GrowthScale
Calculate →
Rule of 40
Growth
A SaaS health heuristic: growth rate + profit margin should exceed 40%. Balances the growth-vs-profitability tradeoff.
Formula
Rule of 40 = Revenue growth rate (%) + EBITDA margin (%)
Good
> 40% combined (top quartile: 60%+)
Scale
Track →
Burn Multiple
Growth
How much cash you burn for each dollar of net new ARR. Emerging as a more rigorous alternative to the Rule of 40.
Formula
Burn Multiple = Net burn / Net new ARR
Good
< 1.5x (great) / < 2x (good) / > 3x (concern)
SeedGrowth
Track →
Magic Number
Growth
Sales efficiency metric: how much ARR you generate per dollar of sales & marketing. Tells you when to step on the gas.
Formula
Magic Number = (QoQ ARR growth) / (Prior Q S&M spend)
Good
> 0.75 (invest more) / < 0.5 (fix efficiency)
