Pick Your GTM Motion

Which go-to-market motion fits your product?

Answer four questions. We'll recommend the best approach.

Question 1 of 4
What's your average deal size?

$1K–$25K ACV — mid-market
$25K+ ACV — enterprise deals
Question 2 of 4
Can users experience value without talking to a human?

Partially — they need some setup or onboarding
No — complex setup, integration, or configuration required
Question 3 of 4
Who is the buyer?
The end user — individual contributor or small team lead
A manager or director — budget holder for their team

Question 4 of 4
What's your current team size?

Market Sizing Framework

The TAM → SAM → SOM funnel

Start broad, then narrow down to the market you can actually capture. Each layer applies real constraints.

TAM
Total

Total Addressable Market

The total revenue opportunity if you had 100% market share with zero constraints. This is the ceiling — useful for investor conversations but not for planning.

Top-Down Formula
TAM = Total customers in category × Average annual spend
Bottom-Up Formula
TAM = (Your price × Potential customers worldwide) × Usage frequency

Example: 500K SaaS companies worldwide × $5K avg annual spend on analytics = $2.5B TAM

SAM
Serviceable

Serviceable Addressable Market

The portion of TAM your product can actually serve, given your features, geography, language, and segment focus. This is your realistic playing field.

Formula
SAM = TAM × Segment filter (geo, size, vertical, language)

Example: 50K B2B SaaS companies in North America with 10–200 employees × $5K = $250M SAM

SOM
Obtainable

Serviceable Obtainable Market

The realistic share you can capture in the next 1–3 years given your team, budget, brand awareness, and distribution. This is your actual planning number.

Formula
SOM = SAM × Realistic capture rate (typically 1–5% in Year 1)

Example: $250M SAM × 2% capture = $5M SOM (Year 1 revenue target)

Bottom-up vs. top-down: use both

The best market sizing uses both methods and triangulates. If they're wildly different, your assumptions need work.

Method Approach Strengths Weaknesses Best For
Top-Down Start with industry reports, % of total market Quick, uses published data Often inflated, misses nuance Investor decks, directional sizing
Bottom-Up Count customers × price × usage Grounded in reality, defensible Slower, requires customer data Operational planning, pricing
Value-Theory Estimate value delivered × willingness to pay Captures new-market potential Most speculative, hardest to validate New categories, disruptive products

ICP Builder

🏢

Company Profile

Define the firmographic attributes of your ideal customer company.

  • What industry or vertical are they in?
  • How many employees? (1–10, 11–50, 51–200, 201–1000, 1000+)
  • What's their annual revenue range?
  • What stage are they? (Pre-seed to Public)
  • What geography? (Region, country, city)
👤

Buyer Persona

Define the individual who champions and/or signs off on purchase.

  • What's their title / role?
  • Who do they report to?
  • What KPIs are they measured on?
  • What tools do they currently use?
  • Where do they spend time online?
🔥

Pain Points

Identify the specific problems that make them a great fit.

  • What problem are they trying to solve?
  • What's the cost of not solving it? ($ or time)
  • What have they tried before?
  • What triggers the search for a solution?
  • How urgent is the problem? (Hair-on-fire or nice-to-have)
✅

Qualification Criteria

The signals that tell you this account is worth pursuing.

  • What tech stack signals fit? (Uses X, has Y integration)
  • What budget authority signals? (Has dedicated team, line item)
  • What behavioral signals? (Visited pricing, signed up for trial)
  • What disqualifies? (Too small, wrong vertical, competitor locked)
  • What's the minimum ACV to be worth a sales touch?

Example ICP: B2B Analytics SaaS

Here's what a filled-out ICP looks like for a product analytics tool targeting mid-market SaaS.

Company
B2B SaaS, 50–500 employees, Series A–C, $5M–$100M ARR, North America + Europe
Buyer
VP Product or Head of Product Analytics, reports to CPO, measured on activation rate and feature adoption
Pain Points
Can't tie feature launches to business outcomes. Currently using SQL queries + spreadsheets. Triggered by board asking for product metrics.
Qualification
Uses Segment or Mixpanel (integration ready). Has 3+ PMs. Visited pricing page ≥2 times. Disqualify: <1K MAU (too small).
TAM Slice
~12K companies match this profile globally. At $15K ACV = $180M SAM.
Channels
LinkedIn (Product Analytics groups), Lenny's Newsletter, ProductBoard community, Product School events

Launch Readiness Checklist

0 / 0 complete
P1

Market & Positioning

Weeks 1–2
  • Complete TAM/SAM/SOM analysis with bottom-up validation
  • Define ICP with firmographic + behavioral criteria
  • Write positioning statement (For [who], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit])
  • Identify 3–5 direct competitors and map differentiation
  • Validate pricing model and initial price points
  • Document 3 key use cases with customer language
P2

Product & Experience

Weeks 2–4
  • Core value proposition is achievable within first session
  • Onboarding flow gets users to "aha moment" in under 5 minutes
  • Billing and upgrade flows tested end-to-end
  • Analytics tracking: activation, engagement, and conversion events
  • Error states, empty states, and edge cases handled
  • Load testing passed for expected traffic
P3

Marketing & Distribution

Weeks 3–5
  • Landing page live with clear value prop and CTA
  • SEO: target keywords in title, meta, H1; core pages indexed
  • Launch blog post or changelog entry drafted
  • Social media launch posts scheduled (LinkedIn, Twitter/X)
  • Email to existing list or waitlist drafted and scheduled
  • Community submissions prepared (Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit)
  • Referral or invite mechanism (if PLG) tested
P4

Sales & Support Readiness

Weeks 4–6
  • Sales deck and demo script ready (if sales-led/hybrid)
  • FAQ and help docs cover top 10 questions
  • Support workflow: who responds, SLA, escalation path
  • PQL definition and lead routing set up (if hybrid)
  • Customer feedback loop: how to collect, triage, and act on it
P5

Measurement & Iteration

Week 6+
  • North Star metric defined and dashboard live
  • Funnel metrics tracked: visitor → signup → activated → paid
  • Week 1 retro: what worked, what didn't, what to double down on
  • First A/B test identified and queued
  • 30-day post-launch review scheduled

6 Go-to-Market Mistakes That Kill Launches

1

Building for everyone instead of a niche

"Our product is for all businesses" means your positioning resonates with nobody. You can't out-market incumbents by being more generic than them.

Fix: Pick one ICP segment. Win it. Then expand. Notion started with students before going enterprise.
2

Confusing TAM with SOM

Telling investors your TAM is $10B doesn't mean you'll capture even 0.01% of it. SOM is what matters for planning — and it requires honest constraints.

Fix: Always present TAM → SAM → SOM together. Use bottom-up SOM for operational planning, top-down TAM for vision.
3

Choosing PLG because it sounds cheaper

PLG isn't free. It requires great UX, self-serve onboarding, usage-based billing, and product investment in growth loops. If your product needs a demo to click, PLG will fail.

Fix: Match your GTM motion to your product's natural complexity and ACV. High-touch products need high-touch sales.
4

Launching without a distribution channel

"Build it and they will come" works in movies. In SaaS, you need a channel strategy before launch day. Product Hunt alone isn't a GTM strategy.

Fix: Identify 2–3 distribution channels where your ICP already hangs out. Build an audience there before launch.
5

No feedback loop in the first 30 days

You launched, got some signups, and... silence. No user interviews, no activation analysis, no iteration. The launch data is the most valuable data you'll ever have.

Fix: Schedule 10 user calls in Week 1. Instrument activation and dropoff. Run your first A/B test by Week 3.
6

Optimizing acquisition before activation

You're spending money on ads to drive signups, but 80% of users churn before experiencing value. You're filling a leaky bucket.

Fix: Fix activation first (onboarding, time-to-value, aha moment). Then scale acquisition. A 2x activation improvement beats a 2x traffic increase.
