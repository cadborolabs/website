02

NPS vs CSAT vs CES, Explained

NPS
Most Popular
Net Promoter Score — measures loyalty and advocacy
The Question
"How likely are you to recommend [product] to a colleague?"
Scale: 0–10
Formula
NPS = % Promoters (9–10) − % Detractors (0–6)
Range: −100 to +100

Strengths

  • Industry-standard benchmark
  • Predicts growth and retention
  • Easy to track over time
  • One question = high response rate

Limitations

  • Doesn't explain why
  • Cultural bias in scoring
  • Lagging indicator
  • Gaming risk if tied to comp
When to send

Quarterly relationship survey to all active users. Best timing: 30+ days after signup, then every 90 days. Avoid sending after negative support interactions.

Calculate NPS →
CSAT
Most Versatile
Customer Satisfaction Score — measures happiness with a specific thing
The Question
"How satisfied are you with [specific experience]?"
Scale: 1–5 stars, or Very Unsatisfied → Very Satisfied
Formula
CSAT = (Satisfied responses / Total responses) × 100
"Satisfied" = top 2 boxes (4–5 on a 5-point scale)

Strengths

  • Granular — measure anything specific
  • Intuitive for respondents
  • Flexible scale and wording
  • Great for A/B testing changes

Limitations

  • Point-in-time, not predictive
  • Scale inconsistency across surveys
  • Recency bias
  • Doesn't predict retention
When to send

Immediately after a specific interaction: post-onboarding, post-support ticket, after using a new feature. Best within 24 hours of the experience.

Track satisfaction →
CES
Best Churn Predictor
Customer Effort Score — measures friction and ease of use
The Question
"[Company] made it easy for me to [do X]."
Scale: 1–7 (Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)
Formula
CES = Sum of all scores / Number of responses
Higher = easier (better). Target: 5.5+ on 7-point scale

Strengths

  • Strongest predictor of churn
  • Directly actionable — reduce friction
  • Identifies UX problems fast
  • Less subjective than satisfaction

Limitations

  • Only measures effort, not delight
  • Requires specific touchpoint triggers
  • Less familiar to stakeholders
  • Harder to benchmark externally
When to send

After task completion: post-checkout, after setup wizard, after support resolution. Triggers on the action, not on a schedule. Best for measuring process improvements.

05

The Feedback Loop Framework

1

Collect

Send the right survey at the right moment. In-app is 3x better than email. Keep it under 3 questions.

2

Categorize

Tag responses by theme: UX friction, missing features, bugs, pricing, support quality. Use consistent taxonomy.

3

Analyze

Find patterns, not anecdotes. What themes repeat across detractors? Which segments score lowest? Where did scores drop?

4

Prioritize

Feed insights into your prioritization framework. Use RICE scores weighted by feedback volume and revenue impact.

5

Act

Ship the fix or feature. Small, fast improvements build trust faster than big, slow ones.

6

Close the Loop

Tell customers what you did. "You asked, we built." This is the step 90% of companies skip — and it's the one that drives loyalty.

07

7 Feedback Mistakes That Waste Everyone's Time

1

Surveying but never acting

You send NPS every quarter, track the score in a dashboard, and... nothing happens. Customers notice. Response rates drop 15–20% each cycle when people feel ignored.

Fix: Every survey cycle must produce at least one visible action. "You told us X, so we built Y" emails drive 2x response rates on the next survey.
2

Asking too many questions

Your "quick survey" has 15 questions across 4 pages. Completion rates plummet after question 3. You get biased data from the most patient (or bored) users.

Fix: 1–3 questions max. One score question + one open-ended follow-up is the sweet spot. Respect your users' time.
3

Wrong survey at the wrong time

You send NPS during onboarding (too early for loyalty), CES after a quarterly review (too late for effort), or CSAT to users who haven't used the feature yet.

Fix: Match the method to the moment. NPS = relationship (quarterly). CSAT = experience (post-interaction). CES = task (post-completion).
4

Treating all feedback equally

One angry power user's feature request gets the same weight as feedback from 200 casual users. You build for the loudest voice, not the biggest opportunity.

Fix: Weight feedback by revenue, user segment, and frequency. 50 users reporting the same issue > 1 user writing a novel about it.
5

Only surveying happy customers

Your NPS is 70 because you only survey users who are active and engaged. Churned users and inactive accounts never get asked. You have a survivor bias problem.

Fix: Include churned and at-risk customers. Send exit surveys. The most valuable feedback comes from people who left.
6

Leading questions and biased scales

"How much do you love our amazing new feature?" isn't a survey question — it's a compliment fishing expedition. You get inflated scores that don't reflect reality.

Fix: Use neutral wording. "How satisfied are you with X?" not "How great is X?" Use validated scales (0–10 for NPS, 1–5 for CSAT, 1–7 for CES).
7

No segmentation

Your overall NPS is 35. Great? Maybe. But enterprise customers are at 55 and SMB is at 10. The aggregate number hides the real story.

Fix: Segment by plan tier, company size, tenure, feature usage, and lifecycle stage. Aggregate scores are vanity metrics without segmentation.
